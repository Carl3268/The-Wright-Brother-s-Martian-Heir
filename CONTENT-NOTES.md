# Story and translation notes

Both editions follow the supplied two-chapter outline. The opening uses a third-person narrator; Ingenuity then speaks directly to the reader. Vietnamese uses **tớ–cậu**, with **mẹ Perseverance** as affectionate personification. English uses **I–you** and **Mum** in the same passages. Quotations name their speakers; Vietnamese quotations are translations.

There are 18 illustrated story spreads, followed by the existing conclusion. The cover and contents are separate views. Desktop folios run from 1 to 38; the compact layout counts 19 reading spreads. The two-month flashback and Allen Chen quotation have their own spreads. Switching VI/EN preserves the current scene.

## Factual edits applied in both languages

- **Timeline:** “Two months earlier” connects the first flight on April 19, 2021, to the February 18 landing. The journey from Earth took about seven months. Confirmation at JPL arrived after the flight, not live.
- **Landing:** The rocket-powered descent stage lowered Perseverance on three nylon cables, with Ingenuity still attached underneath. Ingenuity was placed on the surface later. Mars has too little atmosphere for a parachute alone to land this rover; the sky crane also reduces rocket-plume disturbance around it.
- **Quotation:** “Success is never assured…” is attributed to Mars 2020 entry, descent, and landing lead **Allen Chen**, separately from MiMi Aung’s first-flight statement.
- **Cold:** Nights can reach around −90°C. Sunlit ground and air above it can have very different temperatures. The draft’s exact 23°C/0°C comparison was omitted because it is not a universal surface condition.
- **Flight 6:** A navigation image was lost about 54 seconds after takeoff; subsequent images had incorrect timestamps. The revised text does not blame a simultaneous color photograph. The adjacent Flight 13 image is explicitly captioned as an illustration.
- **Dust:** NASA reported about an 18% reduction in sunlight received by the solar array during the January 2022 storm. The unverified six-day duration, jammed joints and self-cleaning wiggle were removed.
- **Energy:** Heating uses substantial battery energy. The unsupported fixed 25%/75% split was replaced with an explanation that the balance varies with conditions and flight plans.
- **Winter:** The onboard clock reset after an overnight power shutdown. This was not general memory loss, and engineers adjusted the rover’s listening schedule rather than searching for a new radio frequency.
- **Final flight:** Sparse visual features made navigation difficult. NASA’s December 2024 investigation identified a hard landing on a sand-ripple slope and resulting blade loads as the most likely reconstruction. The ice-cream-cone comparison is figurative, not a material description.
- **After flight:** Contact was restored after the final landing. In April 2024 NASA prepared Ingenuity as a **stationary testbed**, storing images, temperature and equipment data locally after Perseverance moved out of range. The book does not claim ongoing Earth contact, a continuing weather-station mission, or confirmed operation today.
- **Legacy:** 72 flights over almost three years, rather than an initially planned maximum of five flights. The ending looks back on the completed flight campaign without implying a new five-year operational milestone.

## Audio

The original 23 MP3 files are unchanged. Some recordings follow an earlier script. Such recordings remain selectable in Voice, with a note that they can differ from the revised text. Device narration is the default on materially different scenes and on scenes without recordings; it requires an installed voice in the chosen language. Aligned recordings are still used by default. Listening remains enabled initially, subject to browser autoplay rules.

The Vietnamese sensor-failure recording is retained as a manually played bonus in Voice because that scene is absent from the new outline. The language edition of the book does not change when playing that explicitly labeled Vietnamese bonus.
